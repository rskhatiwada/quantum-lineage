## The Crux

The book constructs quantum mechanics as the theory of self-adjoint operators on Hilbert space and — the decisive contribution for this library — introduces the *density operator* $\rho$ ($\rho \ge 0$, $\operatorname{tr}\rho = 1$), the entropy $S(\rho) = -\operatorname{tr}(\rho\ln\rho)$, and the analysis of measurement as a two-stage dynamics: unitary evolution (Process 2) versus the non-unitary projection $\rho \mapsto \sum_k P_k \rho P_k$ (Process 1). It also contains the famous no-hidden-variables argument resting on the assumption that expectation values are additive over non-commuting observables, $E(A+B) = E(A) + E(B)$ — an assumption whose illegitimacy Bell would expose in [E1-C06](/library/e1/e1-c06).

## Consensus Shift

Before: quantum mechanics was two competing calculi (Göttingen matrices, Schrödinger waves) with an uncertain logical status. After: one axiomatic theory, and — for thirty years, wrongly — a theorem-shaped belief that hidden variables were impossible. The book's authority delayed Movement 2 by a generation; Grete Hermann's 1935 objection to the additivity assumption was ignored, a documented failure of the community's referee function.

## Notes and Cross-Examination

The additivity flaw is the single most instructive error in the field's history: a true theorem (no *noncontextual, additive* hidden variables) mis-stated as a stronger one. File Hermann's critique under [E1-S03](/library/e1/e1-s03).
