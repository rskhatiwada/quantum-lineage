## The Crux

For the singlet state and spin measurements along unit vectors $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}$, quantum mechanics gives $E(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}) = -\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}$. Assume a local hidden-variable model: $E(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}) = \int d\lambda\, \rho(\lambda)\, A(\mathbf{a},\lambda)\, B(\mathbf{b},\lambda)$ with $|A|, |B| \le 1$ and no dependence of $A$ on $\mathbf{b}$ or $B$ on $\mathbf{a}$. Then for any three settings,

$$
\bigl| E(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}) - E(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c}) \bigr| \;\le\; 1 + E(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}),
$$

which the quantum singlet violates (e.g. coplanar settings at $0^\circ, 45^\circ, 90^\circ$). Locality and quantum mechanics are jointly falsifiable — by experiment, not argument.
