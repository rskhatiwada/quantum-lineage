## The Crux

Three constructions. (i) The identification of entanglement (*Verschränkung*) as "the characteristic trait of quantum mechanics" — not one oddity among many. (ii) The cat: an amplification argument showing the measurement problem cannot be quarantined at microscopic scales. (iii) In the Cambridge papers, the theorem now called steering: for a bipartite pure state, the observer of subsystem 1 can, by choice of measurement, remotely prepare *any* ensemble decomposition of subsystem 2's reduced density operator $\rho_2 = \sum_k p_k |\psi_k\rangle\langle\psi_k|$ consistent with $\rho_2$ — the result rediscovered as the HJW/GHJW theorem in the 1990s.

## Prerequisite Formalism

- Non-uniqueness of density-operator decompositions
- Purification
- The Schmidt form (Schrödinger derives what is essentially the Schmidt decomposition himself)

## Active Replication

**Tier 2.** Implement the steering theorem: for the two-qubit singlet, and then for a randomly chosen entangled pure state of two qutrits, take an arbitrary target decomposition of $\rho_2$ and construct explicitly the measurement on system 1 that realizes it; verify by Monte Carlo over outcomes. Acceptance criterion: reconstructed ensembles match the target decomposition to sampling error. Effort: one day. (This exercise is the kernel of every adaptive protocol built downstream of it.)

## Consensus Shift

Before: EPR was read as a debate about completeness. After (with sixty years of delay): the same mathematics was a constructive theory of remote state preparation. At the moment of publication the Cambridge papers were essentially unread; their modern citation trajectory begins in the 1990s — the cleanest example in the library of content outrunning context.
