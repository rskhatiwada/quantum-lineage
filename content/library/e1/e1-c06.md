## The Crux

A demolition and a discovery. Demolition: von Neumann's additivity assumption is illegitimate for hidden-variable states (expectation additivity for non-commuting observables has no justification dispersion-free); Gleason's theorem [E1-S06](/library/e1/e1-s06) and Jauch–Piron are shown to smuggle in the analogous assumption — *noncontextuality*. Discovery: any hidden-variable theory reproducing quantum mechanics on a Hilbert space of dimension $\ge 3$ must be contextual: the value assigned to an observable must depend on the commuting set measured alongside it.

Acceptance criterion: the exhaustive search terminates with zero valid colorings. Effort: half a day.

## Consensus Shift

Before: "no hidden variables" was a theorem. After: it was two theorems — no *noncontextual* hidden variables (true, Gleason/KS), and no *local* ones (true, Bell 1964), with Bohmian mechanics alive in the complement. The thirty-year sociology of the von Neumann proof became the field's standing lesson in the difference between a theorem and its advertised statement.
