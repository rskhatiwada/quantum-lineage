## The Crux

For an ensemble $\{p_x, \rho_x\}$ encoding a classical variable $X$, the mutual information extractable by *any* measurement obeys

$$
I(X{:}Y) \;\le\; \chi \;=\; S\!\Bigl(\sum_x p_x \rho_x\Bigr) - \sum_x p_x\, S(\rho_x),
$$

hence at most one classical bit per qubit, however cleverly measured. The theorem is the first hard exchange rate between the classical and quantum currencies, and the reason superdense coding (Epoch II) and Schumacher compression (Epoch IV era) are surprising rather than obvious.

## Consensus Shift

Before: no principled limit distinguished quantum from classical information carriage; after: a strict, computable bound, largely unnoticed in the West for over a decade — the Epoch's clearest case of a result waiting for its field to be born.
