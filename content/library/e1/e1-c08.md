## The Crux

The four-setting inequality

$$
S \;=\; \bigl| E(a,b) + E(a,b') + E(a',b) - E(a',b') \bigr| \;\le\; 2
$$

for any LHV model, requiring neither perfect anticorrelation nor ideal detectors — the 1964 inequality re-derived under assumptions an optics laboratory can approximate, together with a concrete cascade-photon experimental design. Quantum mechanics attains $S = 2\sqrt{2}$ (proved optimal by Tsirelson, [E1-S10](/library/e1/e1-s10)).

## Prerequisite Formalism

- Two-outcome observable algebra
- The CHSH operator $\mathcal{B} = A\otimes B + A \otimes B' + A' \otimes B - A' \otimes B'$ and its norm
- Elementary treatment of detection efficiency (the fair-sampling assumption — state it explicitly; it is the loophole that took forty-six years to close)

## Active Replication

**Tier 2.** Compute $\langle \mathcal{B} \rangle$ exactly for the singlet over a full two-angle sweep; locate the $2\sqrt{2}$ maximum; then derive and verify numerically the Tsirelson bound via $\mathcal{B}^2 = 4\,\mathbb{I} + [A, A'] \otimes [B, B']$. Acceptance criterion: the sweep surface with the classical bound plane $S = 2$ and quantum maximum marked. Effort: half a day.

## Consensus Shift

Before: Bell's theorem was regarded (where known) as untestable in practice. After: a costed experimental design existed, and within three years, data. This is the Epoch's terminal node: foundations had become experimental physics.
