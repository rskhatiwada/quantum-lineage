## The Crux

Construct the two-particle state (in modern notation the unnormalized $\int dx\, |x\rangle_1 |x+x_0\rangle_2$, an eigenstate of $x_1 - x_2$ and $p_1+p_2$, which commute). Measuring $x_1$ predicts $x_2$ with certainty; measuring $p_1$ predicts $p_2$ with certainty; by the paper's criterion of reality (a quantity predictable with certainty without disturbance corresponds to an element of reality) and a locality assumption, both $x_2$ and $p_2$ are elements of reality — but no quantum state assigns both. Conclusion as stated: the wavefunction description is incomplete. Conclusion as history rendered it: quantum states of composite systems carry correlations with no local classical model, and the paper had discovered the resource without the theorem.

## Prerequisite Formalism

- Continuous-variable eigenstates and Dirac normalization
- Commuting-observable joint eigenstates
- The reduced-state concept

(The paper's own algebra is elementary; the subtlety is entirely conceptual.)

## Active Replication

**Tier 1 + Tier 2.** Tier 1: show that the EPR wavefunction is a common eigenstate of $x_1 - x_2$ and $p_1+p_2$, and compute the conditional wavefunction of particle 2 after each choice of measurement on particle 1. Tier 2: discretize — prepare the two-qubit singlet, compute the conditional states of qubit 2 for measurements of qubit 1 along arbitrary axes, and verify that the unconditioned reduced state is measurement-choice independent (no-signalling) while the conditional ensembles differ. Acceptance criterion: a plot of conditional Bloch vectors tracing the two distinct ensembles. Effort: half a day.

## Consensus Shift

Before: foundational unease was gestural. After: it was a two-premise argument (reality criterion + locality) whose conclusion the formalism denies, forcing every later position to name the premise it rejects. Immediate impact was significant in print (Bohr replied within months) but null in practice; the paper's citation curve is the canonical example of a fifty-year fuse.

## Notes and Cross-Examination

Einstein privately disliked the published argument (drafted by Podolsky); his preferred version is a pure steering argument. Cross-examine with [E1-S04](/library/e1/e1-s04) (Bohr's reply), whose central passage is famously obscure — documenting *why* it is obscure is part of the entry.
