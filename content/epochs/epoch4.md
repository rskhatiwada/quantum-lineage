**Central mathematical object:** the stabilizer group; the Knill–Laflamme conditions; the threshold theorem.

The crisis was stark: no-cloning appeared to forbid error correction, and continuous errors appeared to require continuous correction. Shor (1995) and Steane (1996) broke both intuitions; Calderbank–Shor–Steane connected quantum codes to classical coding theory; Gottesman (1997) recast everything as the algebra of a stabilizer group inside the Pauli group — the formal language of covariance-matrix and stabilizer-measurement simulations. Knill–Laflamme gave the exact algebraic conditions for correctability; Aharonov–Ben-Or, Kitaev, and Knill–Laflamme–Zurek proved accuracy thresholds. The consensus shift: scalable quantum computation is not forbidden by physics, only by engineering. The mathematics that had to evolve: finite group theory and symplectic algebra over the field with two elements, because errors are discretized by measurement — a fact adaptive-circuit work uses in every cycle.
