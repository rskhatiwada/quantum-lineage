**Central mathematical object:** the Hilbert space and the density operator; at the close of the Epoch, the correlation polytope.

## The Crisis

Matrix and wave mechanics (1925–26) produced predictions without an interpretation of the state. Born's statistical reading of the wavefunction created an immediate tension: if the theory is complete, measurement is not a dynamical process within it; if measurement is dynamical, the theory appears incomplete. Von Neumann (1932) axiomatized the formalism sharply enough that the tension became a theorem-generating machine. Einstein–Podolsky–Rosen (1935) converted the discomfort into a precise dilemma about locality and completeness; Schrödinger, in the same year, isolated the actual culprit and named it: *Verschränkung*, entanglement, together with the phenomenon he called steering. The crisis lay dormant for three decades because it was believed — incorrectly, on the strength of von Neumann's flawed no-hidden-variables argument — to be metaphysical. Bell (1964, 1966) proved it was experimental: local hidden-variable completions of quantum mechanics satisfy an inequality that quantum mechanics violates. Contextuality (Gleason 1957; Kochen–Specker 1967) closed the remaining escape routes on the non-statistical side. In parallel and almost unnoticed, a second revolution began: Szilard (1929) and Landauer (1961) tied information to thermodynamic cost, and Holevo (1973) proved that quantum states carry a strictly bounded amount of classical information. By 1973 the two pillars of the future field existed: entanglement is a physical, testable resource, and information is a physical, thermodynamically accountable quantity. No one had yet multiplied the two pillars together.

## Why the Mathematics Had to Evolve

The EPR argument cannot even be stated in a formalism lacking the tensor product and the partial trace; von Neumann's density operator was therefore a precondition of the crisis, not a response to it. Bell's theorem required a further object foreign to physics at the time: the space of *all* local-hidden-variable correlations, a convex polytope whose facets are inequalities. The migration from operators to convex geometry is the first instance of the field's characteristic move — abstracting away the mechanics and studying the correlations themselves — and it is the direct ancestor of the resource theories of Epoch IV.

## Overview of the Epoch

Epoch I is the only Epoch whose core papers were written by authors who did not know the field existed. It must therefore be read with double vision: what the papers claimed at the time, and what they contained. The Epoch divides into three movements.

**Movement 1 (1926–1935): axiomatization and rupture** — Born's rule, Dirac's and von Neumann's formalisms, then the EPR–Bohr–Schrödinger exchange in a single miraculous year.

**Movement 2 (1952–1969): the crisis made mathematical** — Bohm's explicit hidden-variable theory destroyed the belief that such theories were impossible; Gleason, Bell, and Kochen–Specker then established exactly which kinds are impossible; CHSH made the question experimental.

**Movement 3 (1929, 1961–1973): information becomes physical** — Szilard, Landauer, and Holevo, a thin and initially ignored thread that later Epochs reveal to be load-bearing.

The consensus at the start of the Epoch: quantum mechanics is complete, measurement is primitive, and dissent is philosophy. The consensus at the end: quantum correlations are experimentally distinguishable from all local classical correlations, hidden variables must be contextual and nonlocal if they exist at all, and information processing has physical laws. The delay structure is itself a lesson: Bell's 1964 paper appeared in a journal that ceased publication almost immediately and accumulated citations at a negligible rate for a decade; Landauer's principle waited twenty years for Bennett to make it consequential. Impact at the moment of publication and impact in the lineage are different observables; the library records both.

## Closing Assessment and the Bridge to Epoch II

The Epoch's internal logic, restated as a directed path: axiomatization ([E1-C01](/library/e1/e1-c01)) made the composite-system crisis statable ([E1-C02](/library/e1/e1-c02)), which was correctly diagnosed as entanglement and steering ([E1-C03](/library/e1/e1-c03)); an existence proof ([E1-C05](/library/e1/e1-c05)) reopened the question the axiomatizer had wrongly closed, and the reopening produced the two great impossibility theorems of the Epoch — contextuality ([E1-C06](/library/e1/e1-c06), with [E1-S06](/library/e1/e1-s06)/[E1-S07](/library/e1/e1-s07)) and nonlocality ([E1-C07](/library/e1/e1-c07)) — the latter operationalized within five years ([E1-C08](/library/e1/e1-c08), [E1-S11](/library/e1/e1-s11)). In parallel, the thermodynamic thread ([E1-C04](/library/e1/e1-c04) to [E1-C09](/library/e1/e1-c09)) and the information-theoretic thread ([E1-S09](/library/e1/e1-s09) to [E1-C10](/library/e1/e1-c10)) established that information is physical and quantum information is bounded. Epoch II begins at the confluence: once correlations are resources (Bell/CHSH) and information has physics (Landauer/Holevo), the construction of protocols — money, key distribution, reversible and then quantum computation — is the natural, and historically actual, next move.

Three Epoch-I edges deserve permanent pins on the lineage graph for the adaptive-circuits / Majorana thesis lineage: [E1-C03](/library/e1/e1-c03) (steering) as the conceptual ancestor of adaptive state preparation; [E1-C01](/library/e1/e1-c01) (the Process-1/Process-2 dichotomy) as the ancestor of the unitary-versus-measurement circuit ensembles of Epoch VII; and [E1-C09](/library/e1/e1-c09) (Landauer) as the thermodynamic ledger behind every feedforward bit those protocols consume.
