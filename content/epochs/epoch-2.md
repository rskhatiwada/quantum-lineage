**Central mathematical object:** the unitary (reversible) circuit; the no-cloning theorem as an operational axiom.

Bennett (1973), building on Landauer, proved computation can be made logically and thermodynamically reversible; Fredkin–Toffoli (1982) supplied universal reversible gates. Wiesner's conjugate coding (written c. 1970, published 1983) and Bennett–Brassard (1984) turned the uncertainty principle from an epistemic limitation into a cryptographic resource. Wootters–Zurek and Dieks (1982) proved the no-cloning theorem, the single most consequential "impossibility" in the field: it simultaneously guarantees cryptographic security, forbids naive quantum error correction (setting up the crisis of Epoch IV), and forces the measurement-based, syndrome-extracting architectures that adaptive-circuit work inherits. Aspect's experiments (1981–82) closed the core of the Bell program experimentally. The Epoch's lesson: quantum restrictions are protocol resources.
