**Central mathematical object:** the braid group representation; topological ground-state degeneracy; the anyon.

This Epoch has a long fuse and a short detonation. The fuse: Leinaas–Myrheim (1977) and Wilczek (1982) showed two-dimensional exchange statistics interpolate beyond bosons and fermions; the quantum Hall discoveries (von Klitzing 1980; Tsui–Störmer–Gossard 1982; Laughlin 1983) supplied a laboratory; TKNN (1982) and Berry (1984) supplied the geometry — Chern numbers and geometric phases; Moore–Read (1991) and Read–Green (2000) supplied non-abelian anyons and, in Read–Green, the spinless $p_x + ip_y$ superconductor whose lattice avatar is precisely the class-D target of current simulations. The detonation: Kitaev's toric code and quantum-double models (quant-ph/9707021, published 2003) and Freedman–Kitaev–Larsen–Wang (2002) proved that topological phases *are* fault-tolerant quantum memories and processors, with braiding as computation. Kitaev's one-dimensional chain (2001) reduced the search for non-abelian objects to the search for Majorana zero modes. The consensus shift: error correction can be a phase of matter rather than a protocol. The mathematics that had to evolve: modular tensor categories, topological quantum field theory, and Pfaffian/covariance techniques for free Majorana fermions — the last being the daily language of this thesis lineage.
