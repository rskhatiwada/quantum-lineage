**Central mathematical object:** the symmetry class (the tenfold way) and the topological invariant as a K-theory class.

Altland–Zirnbauer (1997) completed Dyson's threefold way into ten symmetry classes; Schnyder–Ryu–Furusaki–Ludwig (2008) and Kitaev (2009) organized all free-fermion topological phases into the periodic table — the table in which "class A in two dimensions" and "class D in two dimensions" (Chern insulator; $p+ip$ superconductor) and "class DIII" sit as neighbors related by symmetry. Fu–Kane (2008) showed proximity effects manufacture topological superconductivity; Lutchyn–Sau–Das Sarma and Oreg–Refael–von Oppen (2010) reduced the recipe to semiconductor nanowires — the lineage of the Das Sarma group that this academic ancestry passes through — and Mourik et al. (2012) reported the first signatures. The mathematics that had to evolve: random-matrix symmetry classes, K-theory, and the real-space invariants (Chern markers and their descendants) that simulations evaluate on covariance matrices.
